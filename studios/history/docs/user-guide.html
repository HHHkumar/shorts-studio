User guide

Make illustrated history & news explainers

History Studio turns a topic or a script into a narrated YouTube video: illustrated scenes that move, a voiceover, captions, callouts, music and a Short to promote it. This guide walks through every screen, in the order you use them.

Starting the studio

  1. Open the history-studio folder and double-click Start History Studio.cmd.
  2. A black window opens. That is the studio's helper: it saves your work, talks to the AI services and renders the videos. Leave it open while you work.
  3. Your browser opens at http://localhost:5180. If it doesn't, type that address in yourself.
  4. When you're finished, close the browser tab and then the black window.

History Studio can run at the same time as Shorts Studio; they use different addresses.

Your videos

The first screen lists every video you've started, newest first, with its cover picture. Click one to carry on with it, or click ＋ New video.

Everything saves by itself as you work. There is no Save button. You can close the browser at any point and pick up where you left off.

To delete a video, use the small red delete link on its card. This also deletes its pictures and voice clips, and can't be undone.

TipYou can add or change your API keys from this screen too: click Show API keys at the bottom.

A tour of the screen

Once a video is open, the screen has three parts.

Top bar
← All projects goes back to your list. Next to the title, Saving… and Saved ✓ show that your work is safe. The six step buttons let you jump between steps. Steps 3 to 6 unlock once there's a script.
Left: the step
Everything you edit. Each step ends with a Next → button, but you can move back and forth freely.
Right: the preview

Good to knowThe preview is exact. What you see there, frame for frame, is what the render produces, so you can check timing, captions and animation without rendering.

STEP 1 Keys

Paste any keys you have into their boxes, then click Check keys (free). Each key shows works ✓ or problem. Hover over “problem” to see why, for example a mistyped key or missing billing.

You only do this once per browser. After that, go straight to the Story step.

STEP 2 Story

At the top, choose what you're making: 📜 History or 📰 News explainer. The rest of the step changes to suit.

History videos: AI writes it

  1. Pick the Narration language. Picture descriptions are always written in English, whatever the narration language.
  2. Under Topic, type the curious question the video answers, or click one of the idea buttons, e.g. How did the Mughals keep their palaces cool?
  3. Optionally, add an angle, facts to include or things to avoid: “Focus on ordinary families, not pharaohs.”
  4. Choose the voice of the script:
    • Immersive “you are there”: second person, like the reference video.
    • Documentary: a calm, authoritative narrator.
    • Storyteller: follows ordinary people through the story.
    • Playful: witty, with surprising modern comparisons.
    • Deadpan & witty: dry one-liners, a running gag and concrete phrases a picture can land on. Pairs with the ink cartoon look.
  5. Set the Length (1–15 minutes). The hint shows roughly how many words and scenes that makes. Add an audience if you like.
  6. Under Who writes, choose Gemini or Claude and a model, then click ✨ Write the script. A long script takes a minute or two.

The writer returns the script split into scenes, plus a picture description for each scene, a title with alternatives, a YouTube description and tags. It also returns a recurring cast and setting, called the style bible.

Check the factsIf the writer was unsure of anything, a yellow box lists it under Worth double-checking before publishing. Read it before you post. The writer is told to avoid myths and to say “historians think” where things are uncertain.

History videos: your own script

Switch to ✍️ I have my own script and paste the whole script into the box.

  • Leave a blank line wherever a new picture should start. Each block becomes one scene.
  • Long paragraphs are split at sentence ends automatically. Headings and [stage directions] are ignored.
  • As you paste, the box shows the scene count, the word count and roughly how long it will run.

Then choose:

  • ✨ Use script + let AI plan the pictures: your words stay exactly as written, and the AI only suggests what each picture shows.
  • Use script, I’ll describe pictures myself: no AI at all.

Look of the pictures

The style bible box holds the recurring cast and setting, for example: “Neferu, a 10-year-old Egyptian girl with a short black bob and a white linen dress…” It's added to every picture prompt so the same people appear throughout. Edit it freely.

News explainers

News videos are researched live before they're written. The writer only uses what current reporting says, keeps every source, gives each side a fair hearing, and never draws fake pictures of real people. Numbers, places, quotes and people become graphics instead.

  1. Choose the Narration language and the Region / audience (India + global by default; also Global, India, United States, United Kingdom, Europe, Middle East, Asia, Africa or Latin America).
  2. Type the story, or let the studio find one. Tick the categories you care about (Politics, International, Science, Tech, Policy, Business, Health, Climate) and click 🔥 Suggest trending stories. Each suggestion shows how big it is (🔥), a summary and why it matters. Click one to use it.
  3. Add an angle or questions to answer if you like: “What changes for farmers and small exporters?”
  4. Pick a tone: Clear & calm, Energetic explainer or Storytelling. Set the length; 3–6 minutes suits most news.
  5. Click 🔎 Research & write (live search). It searches and cross-checks first, then writes.

When it's done, the step shows:

  • Sources: every article used, numbered, with links. The report is dated today.
  • Check before publishing: anything disputed, still developing or reported by only one outlet.
  • How many scenes became graphics: maps, charts, quotes and so on (see News graphics).
News defaultsSwitching to News also picks news-friendly looks: the editorial illustration style, no colour grade, light grain, tense music and on-screen sources. You can change any of these later.

STEP 3 Scenes & pictures

Each scene is one stretch of narration with at least one picture. This step is where the video gets its visuals.

A scene card

  • The picture (left). Click it to see it large, or drop an image file straight onto it. Underneath are 🎨 Draw (AI, needs a Gemini key with billing), Upload, 🔎 Stock and Copy prompt. Copy prompt is for drawing the picture in any other image tool.
  • The narration: edit the words freely. If the scene was already voiced, it's marked voice out of date until you re-voice it.
  • Picture shows: who is in the frame, doing what, where. If you leave it empty, the picture is drawn straight from the narration.
  • 🎥 Camera move: Auto, slow zoom in/out, pan, tilt up, push in + turn, handheld drift, rack focus, shake (impact), whip in, or still.
  • ✨ Atmosphere: heat haze, floating dust, embers, rain, snow, fog or smoke, fire flicker, or a breeze that sways plants and cloth.
  • ↪ Transition: how this scene arrives. The default, or crossfade, hard cut, ink blot, paper tear, page slide, flash, whip pan or dip to black.
  • The small buttons on the top right move a scene up or down, merge it with the next one (⤓), add a scene after it, or delete it.

Badges on the picture show extra shots (+2 shots), a character layer 🧍, an animated clip 🎞 and callouts 📍.

📽 Storyboard: a new picture every couple of seconds

Fast cartoon explainers change the drawing on almost every phrase. Below the toolbar, choose a new picture every 2, 3, 4 or 6 seconds. The panel shows how many pictures that makes and roughly what drawing them would cost. Then click ✨ AI: storyboard every beat, or use 💬 Storyboard with any chatbot.

  • Each scene is split into beats at its full stops and commas, or before “and”, “but” or “to”. It never splits inside a name or a phrase like “300 million”.
  • Each beat gets its own shot, cutting in on its first word, with a picture description and a stock search. Pictures vary between wide shots, close-ups and single objects, and keep the cast from the style bible. If there's no style bible yet, the storyboard writes one.
  • Pictures you already have stay where they are. A graphic at the start of a scene stays up at least 4 seconds so it can be read.
  • Long videos are planned a few scenes at a time; the button shows which part it's on.

Then click 🎨 Draw N missing or 🔎 Fill N empty from stock, and look through the results. A 9-minute video at 2 seconds is roughly 250 pictures, about $10 to draw.

The ⏱ Longest hold line shows the longest stretch with nothing new on screen. ✕ Remove N empty extra shots takes out shots that still have no picture, to undo a storyboard.

Style referenceMark your favourite picture with ★ Style ref in the Animate editor. New pictures are then drawn to match its look and characters.

Stock photos & videos

Real places and real people often look best as real photos, especially in news. Click 🔎 Stock on any scene, or 🔎 Stock photos & videos for any shot in the Animate editor.

  1. Choose 📷 Photos or 🎬 Videos.
  2. The search box is filled with a suggestion from the writer, like “Red Fort Delhi dusk”. Change it if you like, and press Enter.
  3. Turn libraries on or off with the chips. A 🔑 means that library needs a free key.
  4. Click a result to use it. It's downloaded into your project and placed in the shot. Hover over a video to preview it.

Searching wellUse a place, an object or a person's name, in plain English: “Taj Mahal sunrise”, “clay water pot”, “Indian parliament building”. If a long search finds nothing, the studio tries again with fewer words and says so.
Real peoplePhotos of real people are fine for news and commentary. Never use them in a way that suggests someone endorses something.

The Animate editor

Click 🎬 Animate on a scene to open its editor. Everything is placed by pointing:

  • When: click a word in the narration strip at the bottom. Shots cut, and callouts appear, on the word they're pinned to. If you re-record the line faster or slower, they follow the word.
  • Where: click on the picture.

Chapters

Type a title in the box at the top, e.g. Secret #1: The walls, and this scene opens with a chapter card. Three or more chapters also give you YouTube chapters for the description.

Shots

A scene can hold several pictures. Click ＋ Shot, give it a picture, then click the word it should cut in on. Shots are never closer than a second apart, so nothing flashes by. Each shot has its own picture, camera move, stock and clip.

Camera focus

With 🎯 Camera focus selected, click the important part of the picture. Zooms drift towards it, and the vertical Short crops around it.

Callouts

Pick a kind, click ＋ Add callout, type its text, click where it goes on the picture, then click the word it appears on.

Years before Christ are entered as negative numbers: -1500 means 1500 BC.

Living picture: animated elements

Bring a still picture to life with smoke, steam, fire, lamp glow, birds, clouds, sun rays, water glints, ripples, falling leaves, fireflies or blowing sand. Click ✨ Auto-place (AI looks at the picture) and the AI puts them where they belong. Or choose one from the list, click ＋ Add, then click the picture to place it. Each element has size and strength sliders, and birds, sand, leaves and smoke can travel left or right.

Depth parallax (best for open landscapes): tick it, then click the horizon. The ground then slides more than the sky as the camera moves, which gives a sense of depth.

Character layer (2.5D)

Type who it is (“Neferu, waving”) and click 🧍 Draw character, or upload a PNG with transparency or a picture on a green background. Then click where their feet should be. Choose a motion (breathe, bob, sway, walk in, rise), a size, and flip if needed.

TipDraw the shot's background without that person, then add them as a character. They float in front and move with depth.

Animated clip

A short AI video can play instead of the still. Describe what moves in the motion box, choose 4, 6 or 8 seconds, and click 🎞 Animate (Veo, on your Gemini key, usually $1–3 a clip). Or make the clip in Google Flow and use Upload clip. Tick keep its sound if the clip has audio you want.

Google Flow

With a Google Flow subscription you can animate many shots at once. Click 🎞 Animate in Google Flow:

  1. Tick the shots to animate. Tick opening + chapter starts picks the moments that matter most for your credits.
  2. Click ✨ Write motion prompts (or use the template text), then edit what moves in each shot.
  3. Click 📦 Export Flow pack. You get a folder of numbered start frames, characters and prompts, in order.
  4. In Flow, use Frames to Video: add a frame, paste its prompt, generate. Save each clip under its number, e.g. 03b.mp4.
  5. Back in the studio, click ⬆ Upload clips from Flow and select them all. Each finds its shot by name and plays instead of the still.

STEP 4 Voice

Use an ElevenLabs voice, record the narration yourself, or mix both. Any scene can be redone either way.

🤖 ElevenLabs voice

  1. Pick a Voice and click ▶ Sample to hear it. Warm, low documentary voices suit this format.
  2. Pick a Voice model. Eleven v4 is the most expressive and the default for new videos; v4 Turbo is faster. Both follow voice directions (below).
  3. Adjust if you like. Stability: lower is more expressive, higher is steadier (around 0.4–0.5 suits narration). Also Similarity, Speed (0.7×–1.2×) and, for older models, Style exaggeration.
  4. Click 🔊 Voice N scenes. The line above it shows how many characters that uses and how many you have left this month.

Each scene in the list below has ▶ to play it, ✕ to remove the clip, and ↻ to voice that scene again.

🎭 Voice directions (Eleven v4)

Eleven v4 performs stage directions written into the script in square brackets, right before the words they should change: [amused] They asked to sell pepper. [long pause] Then they stayed.

  • New scripts: when the voice is v4, the writer adds a few directions to suit the tone, such as [deadpan] for witty videos or [hushed] for immersive ones. News only gets neutral ones like [measured]. The Story step says when this is on.
  • Existing scripts: ✨ Add voice directions in the Voice directions box adds them, or use 💬 Add voice directions with any chatbot. Your words are never changed: if the AI alters a word, that scene is left as it was and the note says so.
  • Your own: type them into any scene. Good ones: [warm], [curious], [serious], [amused], [whispers], [sighs], [pause], [long pause]. Use one every two or three sentences, one mood at a time, and describe the voice, not sound effects. An ellipsis… holds a beat, and a dash turns sharply.
  • ✕ Remove all takes every direction out again.

Directions are never shown in captions or spoken as words, and cut points and storyboards ignore them. Older models get the words without them. A scene whose directions change needs voicing again. When you record yourself, the teleprompter shows them in gold as directions for you.

🎙️ Record my own

The studio becomes a teleprompter: the current scene's words are shown large, with the next scene's words underneath.

  1. Click 🎙️ Turn on the microphone and allow the browser to use it. If you have several mics, choose the right one from the list that appears.
  2. Press Space to start recording, read the scene, and press Space to stop.
  3. With Auto-advance on, it moves to the next scene by itself. Use ← → or Prev / Next to move around, and ▶ Play take to listen back.
  • Silence before and after each take is trimmed automatically, and the level is evened out.
  • A quiet room with soft furnishings (curtains, a sofa) sounds far better than a bare one.
  • Keep the mic a hand's width away, a little to the side of your mouth.
  • Read one scene per take.
Exact captionsWith an ElevenLabs key, your recordings get exact word-by-word caption timing, using almost no credits. Without one, the timing is estimated: fine for phrase captions, a little loose for the gold word highlight.
Changed the words?If you edit a scene's narration after voicing it, it's marked out of date. Re-voice or re-record just that scene.

Keeping it moving

Viewers drift when one picture holds for twelve seconds. Something new on screen at least every… (Off, 4, 3 or 2 seconds; 3 by default) fills every longer stretch that has no cut or callout with beats, timed to land on spoken words:

  • Punch-ins: a quick snap zoom onto a detail of the picture, then back out on the next beat. Graphics get a gentler one.
  • Key words: a number or name from the narration (“300 MPs”, “New Delhi”) appears on screen as it's said, with a soft pop. History videos get a comic-style stamp; news gets a clean colour chip. Key words never go over a graphic, the title card or a Short's opening line.

Choose Mix (the default), Punch-ins only, or Key words only. The hint shows the longest hold now and how many beats were added.

More real pictures beat effectsBeats are free and need no pictures, but new pictures help most. On the Scenes step, the 📽 Storyboard plans a new picture every few seconds. Fill them with 🔎 Fill empty from stock or 🎨 Draw missing, then check each one.

Music & sound

  • Pause after each scene: a breath between lines so it never feels rushed.
  • Music volume: keep it low under narration, around 0.08–0.15. Duck the music under the voice dips it while you speak.
  • Sound effects: whooshes on transitions, pops on callouts and key words, a hit on chapter cards.
  • Ambience: wind under heat haze and dust, crackle under fire, rain under rain.
  • Chapter card hold: how long the chapter title shows before the narration carries on.
  • Music: a calm, tense or upbeat bed, none, or your own track (MP3, WAV or M4A; it loops if shorter than the video). Only use music you have the rights to.

Music by chapter (Google Flow Music)

You can give the opening and each chapter its own track. Click ✨ Write music briefs, or use the templates. Copy a brief into Flow Music, generate, download, then use ⬆ Upload track on that row. Each track plays until the next one takes over, with a 1.5-second crossfade. Rows without a track use the main music.

STEP 6 Render & publish

The top of the step warns about anything unfinished: shots with no picture, scenes with no voice, or narration changed after recording. It still renders, so you can check a draft early.

  • Quick draft (720p) takes about as long as the video itself. Watch it through before the final.
  • 🎬 Render final (1080p) takes roughly twice as long. AI clips and heat haze make it slower.

When it finishes, play the video right there, ⬇ Download it, or 📂 Show in folder. A render keeps going if you reload the page or switch steps. Keep the black helper window open.

Make a Short

Cut a vertical 9:16 Short from a stretch of scenes to promote the full video.

  1. Choose From scene and To scene. The length is shown; under 60 seconds performs best.
  2. Write a Hook: big text over the first seconds, like “How did they survive 50°C with no electricity?”
  3. Edit the End card (“Full story on the channel ▶”).
  4. Check it in the tall preview, then 📱 Render Short (1080×1920).

Each picture is cropped around its 🎯 camera focus, so set the focus on the important part in the Animate editor. Chapter cards are skipped in Shorts.

Thumbnail

Three or four huge words, one in a loud colour, over the strongest picture. Pick the picture from the strip, type the Words and the Words in colour, choose the colour and which side the words sit. ✨ Suggest words asks the AI. A shot with a 🧍 character can put them big on the other side. 🖼 Save thumbnail PNG saves a 1280×720 image.

For YouTube

  • Title ideas, each with a Copy button.
  • Description, editable, with buttons to add ⏱ chapters (needs three or more chapter titles), 📰 sources (news) and 🖼 stock credits. Pressing them again updates the lists instead of duplicating them.
  • Tags, ready to copy.
  • The double-check list again, so it's in front of you when you publish.
AI disclosureYouTube asks creators to label realistic AI-generated content. Illustrations usually don't need the label, but AI-animated clips may. Adding “Illustrations made with AI” to the description is a good habit.

Finished files lists every render. 🧹 Delete old takes & replaced pictures frees disk space by removing voice takes and pictures you replaced.

No API credits? Use any chatbot

Every AI button has a 💬 …with any chatbot option beside it. It does the same job through the free Gemini app, ChatGPT or Claude.ai, with no key and no credits.

  1. Click the 💬 button, then 📋 Copy the prompt.
  2. Paste it into the chatbot and send it.
  3. Copy the chatbot's whole reply and paste it into the box in the studio.
  4. Click ✔ Use this reply. It goes through the same checks as an API answer.

Available for: writing the script, planning pictures for your own script, describing every picture, callouts & chapters, the storyboard, news research & writing, placing animated elements, Flow motion prompts, music briefs and thumbnail words.

  • If the chatbot stops part way, ask it to “continue” and paste both parts. They're joined automatically.
  • For placing elements, attach the picture to your chatbot message as well.
  • For news, the chatbot must be able to browse the web.

Four recipes

A fast ink-cartoon explainer

Needs: a Gemini key with billing (about $10 to draw a 9-minute video) and ElevenLabs, or your own voice
  1. Story: History → choose the Ink cartoon look and the Deadpan & witty tone, then ✨ Write the script.
  2. Voice it first, so the beats follow your real timing.
  3. Scenes: 📽 Storyboard → a new picture every 2 seconds → ✨ AI: storyboard every beat. Then 🎨 Draw N missing.
  4. Look: ✏️ Fast ink cartoon. Watch it through in the preview, then render.

A history video from a topic

Needs: a Gemini key (billing on, for drawing) and ElevenLabs
  1. Story: History → ✨ AI writes it. Type the topic, choose Immersive, 8 minutes, then ✨ Write the script.
  2. Scenes: 🎨 Draw N missing, then ✨ AI: suggest callouts & chapters.
  3. Open 🎬 Animate on two or three key scenes: ✨ Auto-place elements and set the camera focus.
  4. Voice: pick a voice, then 🔊 Voice all scenes.
  5. Look: check captions and pace. Render: a quick draft, then the final, a Short and a thumbnail.

Your own script and voice, no keys

Needs: nothing but a microphone
  1. Story: ✍️ I have my own script. Paste it with blank lines between scenes, then “Use script, I’ll describe pictures myself”.
  2. Scenes: 💬 Describe every picture with any chatbot. Then 📋 Copy all prompts into any image tool and ⬆ Upload many pictures, or 🔎 Fill empty from stock.
  3. Voice: 🎙️ Record my own. Space to record, Space to stop, scene by scene.
  4. Render as usual.

Today's news, explained

Needs: a Gemini or Claude key (or a chatbot that browses)
  1. Story: 📰 News explainer → 🔥 Suggest trending stories, pick one, then 🔎 Research & write.
  2. Read the sources and the “check before publishing” notes.
  3. Scenes: graphics are ready. Use 🔎 Fill empty from stock for the photo scenes, and 📽 Storyboard at 3–4 seconds for the long ones.
  4. Voice, then Render. In For YouTube, add sources and stock credits to the description.

Troubleshooting

“The helper is not running”

The black window was closed or crashed. Close the browser tab and double-click Start History Studio.cmd again. Your work up to the last Saved ✓ is safe.

The top bar says “Not saved!”

The helper stopped while you were working. Don't close the tab. Restart the helper; the next change you make saves again.

A key shows “problem”

Hover over the word to see the reason. Usually the key was pasted with a space missing, or (for drawing with Gemini) billing isn't turned on for it.

The 🎨 Draw buttons are greyed out

Drawing needs a Gemini key with billing. Without one, use Copy prompt in any image tool and upload the result, or use stock.

Stock found nothing

Try fewer, plainer words: a place, an object or a person's name. Turn on more libraries. Videos only come from Pexels and Pixabay, which need their free keys.

My recording was “silent”

Check the microphone chosen in the list under the teleprompter, and that it isn't muted in Windows. If you plugged the mic in after opening the page, reload the page and turn the microphone on again.

The captions drift a little on my own recordings

Add an ElevenLabs key. Recordings are then timed word by word, at almost no cost. Re-record or re-upload the scene to apply it.

The character cut-out has a green halo or didn't cut out

The AI didn't draw a clean green background. Click 🧍 Draw character again, or upload a PNG that already has a transparent background.

It feels too busy, or too static

Look → Keeping it moving: try 4 seconds or Punch-ins only if it feels busy. For more energy, use 2 seconds, storyboard at 2–3 seconds, and add callouts on key words.

The render is slow

A 1080p render takes roughly twice the video's length, and longer with AI clips and heat haze. Use the quick draft to check things, and close other heavy programs while the final renders.

My Short is over a minute

Pick fewer scenes. Shorts can now be longer, but under 60 seconds performs best.

Steps 3–6 can't be clicked

They open once there's a script. Write or paste one on the Story step first.

Where files live

Everything stays on this computer, inside the history-studio folder.

FolderHolds
projectsOne file per video: the script, settings and timings
public/projectsEach video's pictures, voice clips, music and animated clips, in a folder of its own
outFinished videos, Shorts, thumbnails and Flow packs
BackupsTo back up all your work, copy the projects and public/projects folders somewhere safe. Copy out as well if you want the finished videos.

Words used in the app

Scene
One stretch of narration, usually one or two sentences, with its pictures.
Shot
One picture inside a scene. Extra shots cut in on a chosen word.
Storyboard beat
A couple of seconds of narration with a picture of its own.
Beat
A punch-in or key word that keeps something new on screen between cuts.
Callout
A hand-drawn label, arrow, date, counter or map mark that appears on a word.
Style bible
The recurring cast and setting, added to every picture prompt so people look the same throughout.
Style reference ★
The picture new drawings are matched to.
Camera focus 🎯
The point zooms head towards, and the centre of a Short's crop.
Living picture
A still with animated elements and depth.
Character layer
A cut-out person floating in front of the picture.
Flow pack
A folder of numbered start frames and prompts for animating in Google Flow.
Ducking
Music dipping under the voice and coming back in the pauses.
History Studio runs entirely on your own computer. Keys stay in your browser. The README.md in the studio folder has the technical details.
